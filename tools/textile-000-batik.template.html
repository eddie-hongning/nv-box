NOUGHTVAULT  //  TEXTILE 000 · THE BATIK PRINTS

The oldest border motif in the archipelago is a keyhole upside down.

A 350 mm hand-drawn square that arrives wrapped around the plate and is never mentioned before it does. Ledger 000 Rev B put batik on the Kit and the Vault and priced it at RM 42. The vendor's shelf ticket says RM 19.90 — so the cost argument is dead, and the answer has to be re-derived from the only thing that still constrains it, which is secrecy.

Internal — never listed · never in What's Included

// 00

What the shop visit changed

Ledger 000 Rev B § 11 corrected Rev A's "batik on every SKU" down to "Kit and Vault only" on the strength of one number: a 45 × 45 cm hand-resist square at roughly RM 42, which is ¥69 against a ¥47 entry-SKU bill. That number came from an estimate. We now have shelf tickets, two production samples in hand, and a vendor.

Four things move. The conclusion does not — but its reasoning does, and that matters, because a conclusion held up by a cost argument falls over the moment costs change, and this one just did.

What survives untouched. § 11's actual insight, which is the reason to keep batik rather than drop it: batik is a resist process — hot wax laid down so the dye cannot reach what is underneath, and what you see is the negative of what was protected. That is a 700-year-old regional statement of this product's thesis, and it is true rather than borrowed. Everything below is an attempt to deserve that sentence.

// 01

The motif — and the turn that makes it ours

Pucuk rebung is the bamboo shoot: a row of tall isosceles triangles that runs across the kepala kain, the head panel of a sarong. It is shared across the region — tumpal in Javanese batik, the same triangle in Terengganu songket, in Bugis weaving, on Peranakan tiles — and it is probably the oldest ornamental band in the archipelago. It means what a shoot means: a thing that is almost entirely potential, growing from nothing.

Then rotate it a half turn.

An inverted isosceles triangle under a circle is a keyhole. It is the keyhole — the one in the NoughtVault mark, the one on the right-hand side of the vault door, next to the struck Ø. The border motif and the logo's keyhole are the same shape, and nobody had to make them so. That is the entire design and everything else in this document is execution.

Why this beats the awan larat option. Enclosure 001 ALT E raised the Terengganu scroll as camouflage and named the tension honestly: it is warm, handmade and ornamental, and the brand is void-black, machined and mute. Pucuk rebung resolves that tension instead of paying it. It is a straight line and a triangle. It is the one traditional Malay motif that is already austere.

// 02

Print 000 · Kepala 24

350 mm finished from a 355 mm cut, with a 2.5 mm rolled hem — the vendor's stock napkin, unmodified. The layout is a sarong compressed into a square: two kepala bands facing each other across a plain badan, framed by a ruled tepi, with the mark alone in the field.

Twelve shoots per band. Twenty-four in total. One per word of a twenty-four word recovery phrase. Nobody will notice, and the one customer who counts them will never be able to stop seeing it.

It counts to 24. It does not store 24. This has to be said in writing now, before anyone reaches for it in copy. The cloth records nothing, encodes nothing, and recovers nothing. The brand has already been caught claiming a plate survives 1,668 °C and calling a seal tamper-evident before the transfer test passed. A textile that hints it is part of the backup would be the third, and it would be the worst of the three, because this one would be charming.

// 03

The mark, in batik language

The medallion is the vault door: a rounded double frame, two solid hinge blocks on the left, the slashed nought standing in for the dial, and the keyhole to its right. The hinges are the only filled mass anywhere on the cloth — everything else is line — which gives the eye somewhere to land in a design that is otherwise all contour.

The keyhole's shaft is a pucuk rebung, inverted, at the same proportion as the twenty-four in the borders. It is drawn with the same block, turned over.

The rule the medallion inherits from Enclosure 001. That document refuses a brand mark on the chengal block, on the grounds that a Ø is a signpost, and on an object whose only defence is being uninteresting the mark is the vulnerability. That reasoning does not forbid the mark here — the cloth is not the hiding place — but it does impose the same rule the steel card gets in 001 § 06: the cloth must never live in the same room as the block. Say it in the onboarding, in one line, without explaining why.

// 04

Colourways

RAKING is the better idea and the worse product, and Batch 001 should not carry it. Black wax on black ground, legible only in raking light, is the most on-brand sentence in § 11 — and it photographs as a black square. Ledger Rev B § 01 flags that the live store currently has no product image on its only product. The one thing Batch 001 cannot afford is a hero object that does not survive a camera. Ship VOID; keep RAKING as the second-year cloth, when there are photographs of something else.

// 05

The wrap — how the surprise actually works

The cloth is not in the parcel. The cloth is the parcel's inside: the plate arrives wrapped in it, four folds and a tuck, no tape and no ribbon. The customer undoes it to get at the thing they bought, and is holding it before they work out that it was not packaging.

That is Enclosure 000's mechanism exactly — the secrecy is the asset — applied to something that costs RM 20 instead of RM 218, ships at 14 g instead of 843, and cannot be seized shut by a European winter.

Why a square and not a sleeve

A wrap gets thrown away. A 350 mm square is a pocket square, a book cover, a lens cloth, a thing on a shelf — it has a second life, and every one of those lives is the brand sitting in someone's house not being a security product. That is the whole gift.

Do not close it with the VOID seal

The obvious move is to seal the last tuck with a seal from SKU E. Don't. Rev B § 06 found transfer already unreliable on titanium; on woven cotton it will not transfer at all. A seal that visibly fails on the wrapper teaches the customer the seal is decorative ten minutes before they use one on the plate.

// 06

Three routes, and one word to be careful with

The vendor is Craft Batik Sdn Bhd at the Penang Batik Factory, Teluk Bahang — the DIY-booking contact is on file. Two capabilities are demonstrated by the samples in hand: a three-colour block-printed napkin in black, grey and white, and a hand-drawn indigo piece with brush-shaded florals. Both at 14 inches, both off the shelf.

Tuliscanting · hand-drawn

Wax drawn freehand with a copper pen. No tooling, no minimum, and every square is measurably different from every other — which is not a defect to apologise for but the reason the Vault tier exists. Slow: reckon a day's work for a handful of pieces, and brief the shop on tolerance, not perfection, because a hand-drawn kepala that meets itself exactly at the corners is a printed one.

Is it batik?  Yes.

RM 16–22est. / pieceno tooling
Capcopper block

Engraved copper strip soldered into a stamp, dipped in wax and struck. Two blocks are needed — one shoot and one medallion — because a 24-triangle bordered layout is not an all-over repeat. Stamp the band along a chalked line and accept ±1 mm of drift; the counter-shoot between each pair is drawn wide enough to absorb it, which is why it is there.

Is it batik?  Yes.

RM 9–13est. / piece+ RM 1,400 tooling
Screenpigment print

Cheapest, fastest, dead flat, and no wax touches it at any point. It is a perfectly good way to make a printed napkin and it is what the three-colour sample almost certainly is. Use it if the volume ever demands it — but then the word on the card changes.

Is it batik?  No.

RM 6–9est. / piece+ RM 300 screens

"Batik" is a process word, not a look. If wax never touched it, it is a batik-motif print and the copy has to say so or say nothing. This is not pedantry and it is not only ethics: the buyer for this product is the same person who screenshots an EMP claim on a passive metal plate, and Malaysia has a national certification regime for exactly this distinction. The brand has spent two claims already. Do not spend the third on a napkin.

// 08

Which SKUs carry it — § 11, re-derived

Rev B kept batik off the entry SKU because it cost 147% of that SKU's bill. It now costs 5.9 points of margin on a product running at 91%. That objection is gone and the answer is still no, for a reason § 11 could not reach while the cost was doing the work.

A surprise that ships on every order is a feature. Features get written into listings, then into reviews, then into the third-party comparison table — and Enclosure 000 already worked out what happens next: the secrecy is a one-time, one-wave asset; it survives exactly until the first unboxing video, and then it converts into the thing that makes the video worth posting. Putting it on the $79 SKU spends that conversion on the customers least likely to film anything.

The seal refill is the interesting case. Rev B § 05 is blunt that SKU E is not a business line — its value is that it is the only reason you will ever have to email a customer twelve months after their one and only order. A flat mailer of ten seals is not a reason to open an envelope. A flat mailer of ten seals and a cloth in this year's colourway is. It costs RM 16 against a $25 ticket, it ships at letter rate regardless, and it turns a consumable into something with a series number. Prototype it against Batch 001's repeat customers before committing.

// 09

The rules, and the two tests before it touches titanium

  • Never in the listing. Not in What's Included, not in the collection copy, not in an alt tag, not in a meta description. Same discipline as the enclosure, for the same reason and with less to lose.
  • Never in the same room as the block. Enclosure 001's rule for the steel card, extended. One line in onboarding, no explanation.
  • The vendor brief stays boring. Napkin batik 14 × 14, corak tempahan — custom pattern. No brand name in the artwork files, no NoughtVault in a filename, nothing in the brief that says what the square is for. The concealment has to hold at the supply-chain layer first.
  • The mark is struck by the shop, not designed by them. Supply the medallion as line art at final size. Batik shops improvise beautifully and this is the one element that must not be improvised.

Test 01 — crocking

New batik gives up dye. A square that transfers indigo onto a plate the customer is about to stamp, seal and photograph is a defect that will be described as a stain. Specify a wet and dry rub test to grade 4 minimum, and a pre-wash and cure before packing. Ask the shop what they already do; most do something.

Test 02 — wax residue

Wax is boiled out at the end of the process, never perfectly. Residue on cotton is invisible and harmless; residue on titanium under a pressure-sensitive seal is an adhesion failure that will be blamed on the seal. Check a finished square against a plate at 40 °C for a week before Batch 001 packs.

One more, which belongs in onboarding rather than in production: cotton holds moisture and titanium does not care, but the space between them does. The wrap is for transit, for the drawer and for wearing. It is not long-term storage in a coastal climate, and it is emphatically not where the plate lives once the customer owns Enclosure 001 — that is what the felted pocket is for.

// 10

What ships with it

Nothing printed goes inside the wrap — that rule is inherited whole from Enclosure 000, where the opening sequence deliberately never travels with the box. The cloth is explained once, afterwards, in the same terminal type as the gate, and only to somebody who already has it in their hands.

Five lines, and the fourth one does all the work. It does not explain the keyhole. The customer either turns a triangle over in their head or they don't, and the ones who do have just been given something no listing could have sold them — which is the same trade Enclosure 000 makes with its three moves, at a twentieth of the cost.

// 11

Six decisions

Tulis or cap for Batch 001?Tulis. At 40–100 pieces the copper does not amortise, so the hand-drawn square is cheaper and the one where no two customers own the same object. Cut blocks when a second batch is committed, not before.

Which colourway launches?VOID. RAKING is the better sentence and the worse product — it photographs as a black square, and the store currently has no product photograph at all. Hold it for year two.

Does the medallion ship?Yes, on VOID and NILA; never on RAKING. Supply it as line art at final size and do not let the shop redraw it.

Twenty-four, or don't count?Count. It costs one instruction to the shop, it is never mentioned in a listing, and it is the detail that makes the object feel designed rather than sourced. The matching rule is that the copy never implies the cloth stores anything.

Does the entry SKU get one?No — but note that the reason has changed from money to secrecy, and write it down that way, because the next person to look at this will find RM 20 on a RM 323 sale and reopen it.

Cloth with the seal refill?Prototype it. It is the cheapest available answer to the one structural weakness in the catalogue — that there is no reason to contact a customer again — and a colourway with a year on it makes a consumable collectable.
